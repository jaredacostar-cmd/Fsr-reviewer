Official Plan Amendment and Rezoning applications to permit a 9 storey condominium apartment building with 161 dwelling units
900 Lakeshore Road West, south side of Lakeshore Road West, east of Whittier Crescent
Owner: 1000570027 ONTARIO Inc.
File: OZ/OPA 25-8 W2

Paul Stewart, Development Planner, provided an overview of the recommendation report, subject lands, application evaluation, key issues, recommendations and next steps.

Councillor McFadden raised concerns regarding stormwater discharge, runoff impacts on Lorne Park Estates and pavement review. Paul Stewart, Development Planner, advised that stormwater would be stored and released at a regulated rate, and that pavement matters would be reviewed.

Councillor Dasko inquired about the wind study, winter wind conditions, the proposed change from low-rise to mid-rise, potential precedent along Lakeshore Road West and Credit Valley Conservation’s review. Paul Stewart, Development Planner, advised that the wind study found the proposal generally acceptable, and that staff did not view the proposal as setting a height precedent.

Councillor Tedjo inquired regarding the survey, property line, grading, runoff and height matters, as well as conformity with the Official Plan Amendment. Paul Stewart advised that Lorne Park Estates had identified a survey discrepancy, planning staff met with the applicant, the revised survey was considered, and the proposal was within the applicable height and policy framework.

John Tummers, Resident, raised concerns regarding the revised survey, land ownership, setbacks and easements, and advised that a final decision should not be made based on the information before the Planning and Development Committee.

Bill Johnston, Resident, raised concerns regarding project viability, housing market conditions and survey matters.

Vinujan Aravinthan, Land Surveyor authorized by Lorne Park Estates, spoke to the survey findings. Paul Stewart, Development Planner, advised that staff would use the identified property line. Hugh Lynch,  Executive Manager, Development South, advised that setbacks are based on the top of bank line and that the lands proposed for transfer to the City of Mississauga were not under dispute.

Edie Kaus, Resident, raised concerns regarding boundary changes, survey information, setbacks, tree protection and tree removal. 

Charlene Haupt, Resident, raised concerns regarding technical gaps, slope stability, geotechnical matters, runoff, pumping, wetland discharge, drainage systems, power failures and coastal wetlands.

Councillor Horneck asked about legal liability. Baiqing Luo, Legal Counsel, Planning and Development, advised that there was no Ontario Land Tribunal mechanism for liability, but a cause of action may be available against the applicant and the owner could potentially be liable if supporting studies were significantly incorrect.

Sue Shanly, Resident, raised concerns regarding wind tunnel effects, tree impacts, wind speeds, the wind study and survey matters. Geoff Russell, Supervisor, Development Engineering South, advised that stormwater release would be controlled through attenuation features and would maintain the existing drainage pattern.

Councillor Damerla asked about construction access, tree removal, control of water release and flooding risks. Geoff Russell advised that consent to enter would not be required, trees would not be removed, and the storage tank and outlet pipe would regulate water release.

Mathew Rutledge, Lawyer and Counsel for Lorne Park Estates, addressed the legal and policy framework and requested that the application be deferred to allow revisions.

Ananya Majumder, Resident, raised concerns regarding site appropriateness, traffic, parking and legal principles, and requested that approval be withheld until issues were addressed.

Mary Simpson, resident, raised concerns regarding climate change, environmental impacts, affordable housing, human impacts and community input.

Arno Schmidt, Resident, raised concerns regarding stormwater, drainage, pumping, land ownership, setbacks, grading and liability. Councillor Damerla sought clarification regarding downstream protections, tank sizing, surface water release, and value engineering. Planning staff advised that the drainage infrastructure would be self-contained and designed to maintain existing drainage features.

 

Councillor Tedjo raised questions regarding drainage, natural water flow, liability, and potential building uses. Geoff Russell, Supervisor, Development Engineering South, advised that the proposed stormwater management system, including a storage tank, pipe system, and surface drainage, would regulate water release and maintain existing drainage patterns, with any larger tank requiring justification. Baiqing Luo, Legal Counsel, Planning and Development, noted that easements would be a private matter and that the Committee's decision is governed by the Planning Act.

Kregg Fordyce, Architect, Studio Deep, advised that the applicant's consultants were available to answer questions. Stefan Gopaul, Associate Director, Windtech Consultants, spoke to the wind study and confirmed that some winter wind comfort exceedances were identified. Kosta Derbish, Urban Planner, Studio Deep, advised that the applicant had engaged with a long-term care operator and that, while long-term care remained an option, the current proposal focused on purpose-built rental housing for retirees and seniors.

Councillor Tedjo spoke to the proposed deferral, resident concerns, provincial regulations, and housing needs. Councillors Butt and Dasko supported a deferral to address outstanding technical issues, particularly stormwater management. Councillor Damerla did not support the application in its current form due to unresolved concerns and overdevelopment. Mayor Parrish thanked all participants.

  • RECOMMENDATIONPDC-0042-2026
    Moved ByCouncillor A. Tedjo

    1. That the application under File OZ/OPA 25-8 W2, 1000570027 ONTARIO Inc., 900 Lakeshore Road West, be deferred to address the concerns raised by the residents.


    2. That eleven (11) oral submissions be received.


    YES (11)Councillor A. Tedjo, Councillor S. Dasko, Councillor C. Fonseca, Councillor J. Kovac, Councillor N. Hart, Councillor J. Horneck, Councillor D. Damerla, Councillor M. Mahoney, Councillor M. Reid, Councillor S. McFadden, and Councillor B. Butt
    Carried (11 to 0)

Official Plan Amendment and Rezoning applications to permit 142 back to back townhomes, one 10 storey apartment building with 241 dwelling units and a public park
2155 Leanne Boulevard
Owner: 1000293648 Ontario Inc.
File: OZ/OPA 26-7 W2

Mila Yeoung, Development Planner, provided an overview of the subject lands, the proposal, the proposed Zoning By-law Amendment, the evaluation of the application, the recommendations and next steps.

Luke Johnston, Dunpar Homes, spoke on behalf of the applicant regarding the development proposal, site context, surrounding area and technical studies submitted in support of the application.

Nick Dell, Managing Director, Harper Dell, spoke on behalf of their client and advised that there are currently no development plans for the adjacent lands. N. Dell noted that shared access and traffic matters support the application, and that further coordination would be required to improve access.

Councillor Tedjo thanked planning staff and the applicant and noted that the proposal aligns with the goals of the Mayor’s Housing Task Force. Councillor Tedjo further noted that the proposal would support a more livable and walkable community and benefit the neighbourhood.

  • RECOMMENDATIONPDC-0043-2026
    Moved ByCouncillor A. Tedjo

    1. That application under File OZ/OPA 26-7 W2, 1000293648 Ontario Inc., 2155 Leanne Boulevard to amend Mississauga Official Plan to Residential Low-Rise II, Residential Mid-Rise and Public Open Space and to change the zoning to RM10-Exception (Back To Back Townhouses-Exception), RA3-Exception (Residential Apartments-Exception) and OS2 (Open Space-City Park) zones to permit 142 back to back townhomes, one 10 storey apartment building with 241 dwelling units and a public park, be approved in accordance with the provisions contained in the staff report dated August 19, 2026 from the Commissioner of Planning and Building, and that staff be directed to return to City Council with the appropriate implementing documents and by-laws for approval.

Official Plan Amendment and Rezoning applications to permit two 16 storey apartment buildings connected by a six storey podium containing 553 dwelling units along with ground floor commercial uses and 34 four storey back to back townhouses
1225 Dundas Street East, northeast corner of Dundas Street East and Arena Road
Owner: Stephen-Mitchell Reality Limited, Whitehorn Investments Limited, Lynrob Investments Limited, Richco Investments Limited and Tobdele Investments Limited
File: OZ/OPA 22-20 W3

Ashlee Rivet -Boyle, Executive Manager, Development Central, presented an overview of the subject lands, site context and proposal.

Alfonso Gesualdi, Resident, spoke in opposition to the proposal and raised concerns regarding construction impacts, dust, traffic and wind. Councillor Fonseca thanked the resident for attending and providing comments.

Councillor Fonseca asked about the construction plan, including whether the applicant would work with the surrounding community on dust mitigation, traffic flow and access, including the elimination of access from Dundas Street. Councillor Fonseca also inquired whether the community would be updated regarding coordination with Dundas Bus Rapid Transit construction and asked for clarification regarding cash-in-lieu of parkland. Ashlee Rivet-Boyle, Executive Manager, Development Central,  advised that the applicant would be required to be mindful of the surrounding community and that the proposal would reduce the number of driveways, with two separate access points proposed through the development.  A.  Rivet-Boyle noted that staff would remain in regular communication and keep the community and Ward Councillor updated, and that construction timelines were not yet confirmed. Ashlee Rivet-Boyle further advised that the City of Mississauga was seeking cash-in-lieu of parkland, with parkland located approximately 600 metres from the property to support a variety of programming opportunities.
