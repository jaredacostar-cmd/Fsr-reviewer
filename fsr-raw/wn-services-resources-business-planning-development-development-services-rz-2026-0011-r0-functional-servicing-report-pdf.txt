INTRODUCTION
1.1. STUDY PURPOSE

This Functional Servicing Report (FSR) has been prepared in support of the 12933 Bramalea Road
Draft Plan and associated Zoning By-Law Amendment planning application to allow for the proposed
development of a residential community on the lands located east of Bramalea Road and south of
Old School Road in the Town of Caledon. The 12933 Bramalea Road lands, also referred to as the
‘Subject Lands’ in this report, are illustrated in Figure 1.1.
The Subject Lands includes a portion of the larger Mayfield Tullamore Secondary Plan (MTSP) Area,
which extends south to Mayfield Road and east to Torbram Road. Preparation of the 12933 Bramalea
Road Draft Plan has been synchronized with the adjacent 12580 and 12552 Torbram Road
properties (currently Mayfield Golf Club), also within the Mayfield Tullamore Secondary Plan.
Functional servicing design of the Subject Lands and adjacent properties were coordinated with the
adjacent properties, referencing the 12580 and 12552 Torbram Road FSR (dated February 2026),
prepared by SCS Consulting.
This FSR, prepared by Urbantech Consulting, provides the grading, servicing, and stormwater
management design required to support the Draft Plan of Subdivision for the Subject Lands. The
purpose of this report is to demonstrate that the development can be graded and serviced in
accordance with the Town of Caledon, TRCA, and MECP design criteria.
1.2. BACKGROUND & SUBWATERSHED STUDIES
1.2.1. Mayfield Tullamore Local Subwatershed Study
The MTSP Area consists of approximately 612 ha within the Peel Region’s Settlement Area
Boundary Expansion (SABE) Study Area. The participating landowners make up approximately 438
ha (71.5%) of the total MTSP Area. The Town of Caledon is undertaking the Mayfield Tullamore
Local Subwatershed Study (MTLSWS) in support of the planning framework and urban structure
proposed for the MTSP Area within the West Humber River subwatershed.
While the MTLSWS is ongoing, the Phase 1 and Phase 2 reports are available to inform FSR work
at the Draft Plan level. The Phase 1 Report, prepared in February 2025, characterized existing
conditions, including natural heritage features, hydrologic features, and surface / groundwater
systems within study area. The Phase 2 Report prepared in April 2025 introduced the preferred land
use plan (of the MTSP) and included analysis, impact assessment, mitigation, and recommendations
for stormwater management requirements for future SWM facilities on major reaches in the West
Humber River subwatershed.
This FSR builds on the recommendations of the MTLSWS Phase 2 report, including quantity, quality,
erosion control, and water balance criteria. The Phase 3 report will develop the detailed SWM
strategy, including end-of-pipe facilities and low impact development (LID) measures to meet these
objectives. When the Phase 3 report is available, its recommendations will be assessed and the
12933 Bramalea Road FSR will be updated as needed.

STORMWATER MANAGEMENT STRATEGY
3.1. EXISTING CONDITIONS

Existing (pre-development) overland flow within the subject site is split between the West Humber
Tributaries 4(3)4-1 and 4(3)2-1, within the TRCA Humber River subwatershed, Basin 36.
The West Portion of the site drains to the West Humber River Tributary 4(3)4-1. The North/Central
portion of the site drains through an existing 450mm culvert and overland to West Humber Tributary
4(3)2-4.
The East portion of the site drainage to West Humber Tributary 4(3)3-1. West Humber River
Tributaries 4(3)2-4 and 4(3)3-1 converge and discharge to Tributary 4(3)2-1.
Tributary 4(3)2-1 subsequently joins Tributary 4(3), which conveys flows downstream toward
Tributaries 4(2) and 4(1), together with the City of Brampton Block 48-2 Tributary E.
Pre-development drainage catchments, overland flow direction and ultimate discharge locations are
shown on Drawing 3.1.
TRCA originally created the Humber River Watershed Hydrology model (Visual OTTHYMO). GEI
Consultants conducted a local subwatershed study, in collaboration with SCS Consulting Group in
2025, and provided the LSS Visual OTTHYMO model for the study area to the TACC Caledon study
team (Urbantech Consulting) in 2026. This model forms the basis of the hydrologic analysis.
3.1.1.Geotechnical / Hydrogeological considerations
GEI Consultants Canada Ltd. completed a Geotechnical Investigation and Report dated March 25,

recommendations for the Subject Lands. Refer to Appendix B for the GEI reports.
Geotechnical Conditions
The geotechnical report indicates that the site is generally underlain by topsoil, cohesive glacial till,
layered clay and silt, cohesionless sand/silt/gravel deposits, and lower cohesionless glacial till. These
conditions are generally considered manageable for the proposed subdivision development;
however, several geotechnical and constructability considerations will need to be addressed through
detailed design.
Key geotechnical considerations include the depth and constructability of the proposed sanitary
servicing, particularly the deep trunk sanitary sewer and the crossing below West Humber Tributary
4(3)4-1. The sanitary sewer may be located at significant depths below existing grade, and the
tributary crossing may require specialized construction methods, potentially including trenchless

Hydrogeological Conditions
The hydrogeological report indicates that groundwater levels vary across the site, with local
groundwater flow generally toward the southeast, toward tributaries of the West Humber River. The
site is within the jurisdiction of the TRCA and the CTC Source Protection Region.
From a source water protection perspective, the GEI report indicates that the site is not located within
a Wellhead Protection Area or Intake Protection Zone. However, the northwestern portion of the site
is partially located within a Highly Vulnerable Aquifer area, and the site is located within a Significant
Groundwater Recharge Area. These designations do not prevent development, but they require
appropriate consideration of groundwater recharge, water quality protection, infiltration feasibility,
and construction-phase groundwater management.
Construction dewatering is expected to be required for portions of the development, particularly for
deeper servicing, the SWM facility, and the sanitary sewer crossing. The hydrogeological report
identifies preliminary dewatering rates and notes that dewatering approvals may be required
depending on final construction methods, excavation depths, groundwater levels, and anticipated
pumping rates. Dewatering discharge may also require quality monitoring and potential treatment
prior to discharge, subject to final discharge location and applicable approval requirements.
The hydrogeological report also includes a preliminary water balance assessment. The assessment
indicates that, without mitigation, post-development conditions would reduce infiltration and increase
runoff relative to existing conditions.
As a result, stormwater management and low impact development (LID) measures may be required
to maintain water balance objectives, where feasible. A preliminary LID plan to address the infiltration
deficit under post-development conditions is discussed further in Section 3.5.

3.2. Proposed Drainage
The proposed drainage system for the Subject Lands consists of a minor storm sewer system and a
major overland flow system. The proposed storm sewer and overland flow drainage patterns are
illustrated on Drawing 3.2. The storm sewer design sheets are provided in Appendix C.
3.2.1.Minor System
The minor storm sewer system has been designed to convey the 10-year storm event in accordance
with the Town of Caledon design standards and the applicable IDF parameters. Flows in excess of
the 10-year storm event will be conveyed overland through the major drainage system to the
designated stormwater management (SWM) facilities and outlet locations.
The storm sewer inverts at the SWM facility inlets will be established such that the storm sewer
system is not surcharged during the 10-year storm event, taking into consideration the corresponding
extended detention water level in the receiving SWM facility. This will be confirmed during detailed
design through a hydraulic grade line analysis.
3.2.2.Major System
The major drainage system will consist of the proposed internal road network and overland flow
routes. The road network will be graded to safely convey excess runoff up to the 100-year storm
event within the proposed right-of-way limits and toward the designated SWM facilities and outlet
locations.
The major system flow routes and drainage directions are shown on Drawing 2.1 and Drawing 7.1.
The capacity of the major system will be confirmed during detailed design through overland flow
analysis, once final grading and servicing plans are available. The major system flow width will not
impact capacity, since the drainage areas are relatively small and additional capture can be provided
to ensure that the road capacity is not exceeded.

3.3.2.Stormwater Management Criteria
The stormwater management requirements for the Subject Lands are based on the criteria
established through the Mayfield Tullamore Local Subwatershed Study and applicable TRCA, Town
of Caledon, and CLI-ECA requirements.
The Subject Lands ultimately outlet to the West Humber River system, including West Humber
Tributary 4(3)4-1, Tributary 4(3)3-1, and Tributary 4(3)2-1, where the TRCA criteria for Humber River
subwatershed Basin 36 applies.
The applicable criteria include water quality control, erosion control, quantity control, Regional Storm
control, and water balance requirements, as summarized below.
Water Quality Control
The West Humber River tributaries require Enhanced Level 1 water quality control, equivalent to
long-term removal of 80% total suspended solids, in accordance with the MOE Stormwater
Management Planning and Design Manual, 2003.
For the Subject Lands, water quality control will be provided through a treatment train approach,
including end-of-pipe SWM facilities and LID measures, where feasible. The proposed strategy will
also consider the Town of Caledon’s CLI-ECA stormwater management criteria, which prioritizes
retention, LID filtration, and conventional stormwater management in that order.
Erosion Control
Erosion control targets for the West Humber River were established through the Mayfield Tullamore
Local Subwatershed Study. The erosion control criteria is to release the 25 mm runoff volume from
the proposed SWM pond over an extended detention of 48 hours. As per the TRCA SWM criteria
and the Mayfield Tullamore LSWS, an additional on-site retention of 5 mm is required for erosion
control.
Quantity Control
As identified in the Phase 1 Mayfield Tullamore Local Subwatershed Study, prepared by GEI
Consultants, the Subject Lands are located within the Humber River watershed, Sub-Basin 36.
The Humber River hydrology assessment considered both the 6-hour and 12-hour AES storm
distributions for the 2-year through 100-year storm events. The 6-hour AES storm distribution was
adopted for quantity control design, as it resulted in more conservative peak flows and larger quantity
control requirements. The Regional Storm was assessed using the last 12 hours of the Hurricane
Hazel storm distribution under AMC III conditions.
The allowable release rates for the 2-year through 100-year storm events were established based
on Unit Flow Equation F for Humber River Sub-Basin 36, as identified in Table E.1 of the TRCA
Stormwater Management Criteria. The Regional Storm allowable release rate was established based
on the existing Regional Storm peak flow rate to each outlet.

The SWM pond facilities will have multiple outlet components, including extended detention, flood
flows, emergency spillway and a maintenance drawdown pipe or sump (where applicable). The pond
outlet details will be determined at the detailed design stage.
The extended detention pipe will consist of a reverse-slope bottom draw pipe extending from the
bottom of the pond to an orifice plate on the control structure. The submerged end of the proposed
bottom draw pipe will be fitted with a perforated pipe section of sufficient area of opening and will be
protected by a gravel jacket. The orifice plate will be sized to meet the required extended detention
flows and required drawdown time. The orifice plate will be bolted onto the outlet structure with the

control by debris, etc., a minimum orifice size of 75 mm is desirable.
A series of orifices or a compound weir knock-out will be designed at the detailed design stage for
the pond outlet control structure in order to achieve the quantity control target flows.
The conveyance of emergency flows (i.e., uncontrolled Regional flows) through the SWM pond
facilities will be accommodated by emergency outlet structures within the pond set above the
Regional pond operating water level, in the event that all outlet structures within the SWM pond are
blocked. The emergency conveyance details will be provided at the detailed design stage for the
proposed SWM facilities.

3.4.5.Pond Naturalization
The main purpose of stormwater management is to maintain watershed hydrology and protect the
ecological health and integrity of downstream waterbodies by providing the flood, erosion and water
quality controls necessary to protect downstream ecosystems. In addition to these functions, SWM
facilities can complement and support adjacent natural heritage systems and their ecological
functions. As this green infrastructure is typically constructed adjacent to valley and creek corridors
which form part of natural heritage systems, they can enhance linkage and buffering functions and
opportunities to improve the condition quality of adjacent natural features. Many of these functions
can be achieved by naturalizing components of the SWM facilities by planting a diversity of locally
native trees, shrubs and groundcovers.
The use of native species in naturalization is beneficial to maintaining and enhancing local
biodiversity. Often times, adjacent natural heritage systems are in a degraded ecological state and
have lost much of their native biodiversity, so providing a source of native seed nearby can help to
restore these systems.
Consistent with the Stormwater Management Facility Planting Guidelines (CVC 2003) and Thermal
Impacts of Urbanization including Preventative and Mitigation Techniques (CVC 2011), it is
recommended that locally native tree, shrub and groundcover species be utilized for landscaping the
proposed SWM facilities.
The purpose of inlet and outlet plantings is two-fold:
•

To provide shading of incoming and outgoing water; and

•

To help prevent substrate erosion.

Numerous native trees and shrubs can be utilized for these areas including, trembling aspen, largetoothed aspen, red maple, sugar maple, speckled alder, red-osier and grey dogwood, nannyberry,
and numerous native species of willow.
Landscaping plans for the proposed SWM pond facility will be prepared at detailed design.
The timing of SWM pond landscaping is dependent on timing of development. Plant species that are
dependent on the permanent pool elevation will not be installed until it is confirmed that the SWM
facility functionally maintains the permanent pool elevations. Further, no planting of any proposed
aquatic species in the sediment forebay and/or areas of the permanent pool will be undertaken until
after initial cleaning and subsequent assumption.

3.5. SITE WATER BALANCE AND LOW IMPACT DEVELOPMENT
In addition to meeting the quantity, quality, and erosion control targets, the proposed SWM strategy
will address water balance requirements for the subject lands. The site water balance aims to mimic
pre-development groundwater recharge rates in order to maintain groundwater as a source of flow.
3.5.1.Site Water Balance
A site water balance assessment was conducted by GEI as part of the Hydrogeological Investigation
(dated June 2026), in order to determine local pre-development annual infiltration volumes, impacts
of proposed development to annual infiltration volumes, and the impact of potential mitigation
measures to preserve annual groundwater recharge under post-development conditions.
Development of an area affects the natural water balance. The most significant difference is the
addition of impervious surfaces as a type of surface cover (i.e., roads, parking lots, driveways, and
rooftops). Impervious surfaces prevent infiltration of water into the soils and the removal of the
vegetation removes the evapotranspiration component of the natural water balance. The evaporation
component from impervious surfaces is relatively minor compared to the evapotranspiration
component that occurs with a healthy vegetation cover. The net effect of the development of a
property is expected to be an increase in the water surplus resulting in a decrease in infiltration and
an increase in runoff.
Per the water balance assessment prepared by GEI, the subject development will result in a
decreased annual infiltration of 63,487 m3/year (-66%), as compared to pre-development conditions.

3.5.2.Low Impact Development (LID) Plan
LID measures are recommended, where practical, in order to promote infiltration and mitigate the
overall infiltration deficit resulting from post-development conditions. While end of pipe facilities
provide the minimum required SWM controls, the use of LID (Low Impact Design) stormwater
management measures can be helpful to reduce the amount of runoff by increasing on-site retention,
infiltration, and evapotranspiration. The use of LIDs in a “treatment train” approach has long been
endorsed by the TRCA.
There are many LID measures available for use. Techniques to maximize the water availability in
pervious areas, such as designing grades to direct roof runoff towards open space areas throughout
the development, where possible (e.g., yards, boulevards, landscaped areas, swales, green space
in parking lots, etc.), which can increase recharge in the developed area. Where possible, increasing
topsoil depths in the pervious areas to retain more water in storage can also assist to reduce runoff
volumes and increase the potential for infiltration. Other engineered LID measures such as infiltration
and/or exfiltration trenches, HDFs, enhanced grass swales, and bioswales can be used to reduce
runoff volumes and increase the potential for infiltration.
Drawing 3.5 shows the interpreted depth of groundwater throughout the subject lands, as well as
preliminary preferred locations for LID measures. LID placement is typically best in areas where
groundwater is at least 2+ m below proposed grade. As shown on Drawing 3.5, there are areas
within the subject lands where high groundwater restricts potential locations for LID measures.
The preliminary water balance mitigation plan includes the following LID measures:
•

Park LID: An infiltration gallery is proposed within the northeast park, as shown on Drawing
3.5. These are rectangular trenches lined with geotextile fabric, filled with clean granular

CONCLUSION

This Functional Servicing Report has been prepared in support of the proposed development at
12933 Bramalea Road in the Town of Caledon. The report confirms, at a functional level, that the
Subject Lands can be graded, serviced, and provided with stormwater management infrastructure in
accordance with applicable Town of Caledon, Region of Peel, TRCA, CLI-ECA, and Mayfield
Tullamore Local Subwatershed Study requirements.
•

The proposed grading strategy provides a coordinated framework for development of the
Subject Lands. The preliminary grading has been developed to maintain positive drainage,
match existing and future boundary conditions along Bramalea Road and Old School Road,
provide suitable interfaces with adjacent lands including the Mayfield Golf Club Inc. lands,
and accommodate municipal servicing, stormwater management, overland flow routes, and
future development connections.

•

The geotechnical and hydrogeological investigations completed by GEI Consultants Canada
Ltd. identify no single constraint that precludes development of the Subject Lands. However,
the reports identify several items requiring further review through detailed design, including
deep sanitary sewer construction, the sanitary sewer crossing below West Humber Tributary
4(3)4-1, construction dewatering, SWM pond liner requirements, groundwater protection,
and water balance/LID requirements. Additional coordination with GEI is ongoing with
respect to the site water balance, which may inform the final LID strategy, including the type,
location, feasibility, and sizing of infiltration or retention-based measures.

•

The proposed storm drainage system consists of a minor storm sewer system and a major
overland flow system. The minor system has been designed to convey the 10-year storm
event, while flows exceeding the minor system capacity will be conveyed overland through
the proposed road network to the designated stormwater management facilities and outlet
locations. The major system capacity, storm sewer hydraulic grade line, and final inlet/outlet
elevations will be confirmed through detailed design.

•

The stormwater management strategy includes three SWM facilities serving the Subject
Lands and contributing external drainage areas. SWM Facility 1 is located within the Subject
Lands, while SWM Facilities 3 and 4 are located to the south within the Mayfield Golf Club
Inc. lands and were previously designed by SCS Consulting. Urbantech’s current modelling
has refined the drainage area and imperviousness assumptions for the TACC Caledon lands
and confirms that the proposed SWM strategy is intended to provide the required water
quality, erosion, quantity, Regional Storm, thermal mitigation, and CLI-ECA water balance
controls.

•

SWM Facility 1 has been functionally designed as a wet pond facility and provides the
required permanent pool, extended detention, and active storage volumes for the applicable
storm events. The proposed pond outlet controls have been sized to meet the required
erosion and quantity control release rates, and the proposed drawdown time satisfies the
erosion control target. The proposed SWM strategy also demonstrates that postdevelopment peak flows are controlled at the identified downstream outlet locations.
