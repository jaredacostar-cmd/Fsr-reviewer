INTRODUCTION

This Functional Servicing and Stormwater Management Report is provided in support of a proposed site
plan located in the Town of Caledon. The objective of this report is to provide a servicing plan for the 2.6
ha property referred to as “’0’ Humber Station Road”, located north of King Street and east of Humber
Station Road. The subject site is registered as part of Lot 11, Concession 5, Town of Caledon within the
Regional Municipality of Peel. Please refer to Figure 1.1 for the location plan.
This document will summarize the proposed municipal servicing scheme in regards to potable water,
sanitary flows, and stormwater management. The following sections provide information regarding the
proposed services for the subject development while ensuring compatibility with the existing
infrastructure and previous planning resolutions.

WATER SUPPLY SERVICING

This section describes how the proposed water distribution plan is able to adequately supply potable
drinking water for the proposed development.

The subject site is located just outside of Pressure Zone 6 (PZ6). PZ6 is able to service elevations up to 262
masl. The Caledon Station Secondary Plan (CSSP) identifies that the proposed ground elevations of the
Subject lands area too high for adequate long-term servicing by the PZ6 servicing system (Urbantech
2024). The ultimate servicing will be provided through a new Pressure Zone 7 (PZ7), which would service
lands between elevations of 262 – 250 masl. Interim alternative water servicing arrangements are being
investigated at the secondary plan level. Some of these alternatives include such a proposed booster
station at King and Coleraine Drive as well as an elevated tank that would be located at the north west
corner of the CCSP lands. Details of the external servicing approach will become available further along in
the project design.
A 400 mm watermain along Humber Station is proposed to supply water to the subject lands (Urbantech
2024). Figure 3.1 depicts the proposed future watermain along Humber Station Road.

The water servicing system was designed in accordance with the Region of Peel Public Works Watermain

CLI-ECA Requirements
•

Developments within the Town are required to comply with CLI-ECA requirements. The CLI-ECA
mandates controlling at least 27 mm through retention methods (such as infiltration and reuse),
followed by filtration for any remaining amount. Conventional water quality control measures are
permitted only after maximizing retention and filtration.

The following sections outline the proposed stormwater management solutions to be implemented on
the subject site, ensuring compliance with the applicable stormwater management criteria detailed in
Section 5.2. Stormwater will be managed using a dual drainage system. The minor system, consisting of
sewers, will be designed to capture and convey flows from a 10-year storm event. The major system,
comprising of overland flow routes, will handle excess flows beyond the capacity of the minor system.
Preliminary groundwater levels recorded from the borehole log results indicate that the groundwater
table is relatively high (See the Groundwater Contour Map in Appendix A). High groundwater levels limit
the feasibility for infiltration and retention technologies to be employed on site. It is possible that there
may be seasonal lows in groundwater depth that may allow for the application of some technologies for
parts of the year, however further investigation would be needed to confirm if such conditions exist. Upon
further study, infiltration and retention technology applications may be revisited.
The CSSP outlines that drainage areas leading to the Main Humber River watershed do not require
quantity controls (Urbantech 2024). The subject site is graded to maintain the existing topographic
direction towards the northeast.
Quantity and quality control for Site Plan 1 would be provided through two underground storage tanks
and storm filter unit. The tanks will store up to the 100-year volume to control to the 10-year storm sewer
flow.

A water balance assessment was conducted to characterize the water entering and existing the site area.
Through the Ontario Ministry of the Environment, Conservation and Parks (MECP) Source Protection
Information Atlas, it is determined that the subject site is not located within a Well Head Protection Area
(WHPA) or a Significant Groundwater Recharge Area (SGRA). As a result, the proponent is not required to
maintain the existing water balance, however efforts to achieve it will be investigated.
Additional groundwater monitoring data is expected to support the design of the project at the detailed
design phase. At present, the groundwater monitoring data shows high ground water levels relative to
the site ground levels, reducing the feasibility for any infiltration efforts. As well, the site soils are
comprised of silty clay material, which is unfavourable for infiltration. As more site-specific monitoring
data is made available, the feasibility for infiltration may be revisited.
A site water balance analysis was completed and provided in Appendix D. Based on the analysis, the predevelopment site conditions have a runoff of 4,501 m3/year, infiltration of 5,501 m3/year, and
evapotranspiration of 12,445 m3/year.

management, which can be achieved through a hierarchical approach to provide flexibility in the
implementation of measures to meet the runoff volume target. The runoff volume control target
hierarchy has the following order:
•

Control hierarchy priority 1 (Retention),

•

Control hierarchy priority 2 (LID filtration), and

•

Control hierarchy priority 3 (Conventional Treatment).

The subject site has underlying silty clay and high groundwater levels which limits the opportunity for
infiltrating stormwater for retention and infiltration trenches. Instead, opportunities for site plan
retention and filtration will be revisited later on in the design process.

In order to mitigate the effects of construction on site, a comprehensive ESC strategy following the Erosion
and Sediment Control Guideline for Urban Construction by Sustainable Technologies is outlined for the
project. This ESC Plan provides a framework to be further refined at a later stage of the design process.
Erosion Control Measures
•

Phasing: It is advantageous to reduce the limit of construction during the topsoil stripping phase
in order to limit areas with soil exposure.

•

Stabilization: Grading for construction generally requires the removal of topsoil immediately. If
piled or stored on site, it must be stabilized immediately. It is also recommended that areas left
u-worked for longer than 30 days be stabilized. Possible measures are as follows:

•

o

Mechanical Seeding/Hydroseeding

o

Terra Seeding

o

Growth Media Blankets

Cut-off Swales: To be designed as per ESC guidelines and stabilized accordingly.

Sediment Control Measures
•

Siltation control perimeter fencing
o

Double Silt Fence with Straw Bales

o

Siltation Control Fence

•

Cut-off Swales: To be designed as per ESC guidelines and stabilized accordingly.

•

Flow distributors: Silt Socks have been proposed as they provide ponding opportunities and
energy dissipation, helping to slow down flow velocity and promote sediment settling in swales.

•

Sediment Traps: Have been sized in accordance with ESC guidelines.

•

Temporary Sediment Ponds: Shall be sized as per MECP Quality control guidelines. A design
drawdown time of 48 hours is required.

CLOSING REMARKS

This Functional Servicing Report has been prepared to demonstrate the conformance of the proposed
storm, sanitary, and water supply servicing concept for the subject residential development with the
Region of Peel, TRCA, and Town of Caledon Design Standards.
Water Supply Servicing
•

The proposed development will ultimately be serviced by Pressure Zone 7 to ensure adequate
water supply and pressure. The construction of a booster station at King Street and Coleraine
Drive would be able to service the site as an interim solution for water supply.

•

A 400 mm transmission watermain proposed along Humber Station Road will provide connectivity
to the municipal system.

Sanitary Servicing
•

Sanitary flows will be directed through a sewer along the public ROW and directed north and
across to Humber Station Road.

•

The ultimate servicing strategy will utilize the new proposed trunk sewer on Humber Station Road.

Storm Servicing
•

Stormwater will be managed through a dual-drainage system, with minor flows conveyed via
sewers and major flows through overland routes.

•

High groundwater levels are noted which limits the feasibility for retention and infiltration on
site. Further monitoring results will inform the project design.

•

Site Plan 1 will achieve quantity and quality control through a storage tank and filter unit.

•

The Public ROW will drain uncontrolled towards the outlet. Quality control is to be provided by a
filter unit upstream of the outlet culvert, to be designed and implemented by others.

•

Site Plan 2 will achieve quantity and quality through underground tanks and filter units located
in the P1 level parking garage. The tanks will also be designed for rainwater re-use.
